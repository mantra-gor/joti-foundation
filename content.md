About Joti Foundation

Joti Foundation is a youth-led, women-led [both chairperson and CEO considered] disaster resilience non-profit organisation focused on saving lives, strengthening communities and building resilience across the complete Disaster Management Cycle. The cycle includes Prevention and Mitigation, Preparedness, Response and Recovery. Guided by the belief that every minute matters during a disaster, JF works to empower and equip local youth to become first responders capable of delivering timely rescue and relief in the critical 25 hours after a disaster, while bridging the physical connection needed to reach disaster-affected communities.
JF's approach is rooted in community ownership and strong partnerships with government systems, particularly the National Disaster Response Force (NDRF), State Disaster Response Forces (SDRFs) and local authorities. Drawing on its experience during the Punjab floods, where its youth teams and rescue boats enabled access to isolated communities and supported relief operations alongside NDRF, JF is building a model that can be replicated across disaster-prone regions of India.
Beyond emergency response, JF invests in long-term resilience through youth leadership, livelihood opportunities, community preparedness and innovative programmes such as children's eye health, recognising that healthy, skilled and empowered communities are better prepared to withstand future disasters. During the period 2026–2036, JF will initially focus on North Eastern states, Uttarakhand, Himachal Pradesh and Punjab, while expanding preparedness efforts to other vulnerable states and contributing grassroots learning to strengthen national disaster management policies and practices.
Needs Assessment

India is among the world's most disaster-prone countries, facing recurring floods, earthquakes, landslides, cyclones and other hazards that disproportionately affect vulnerable communities. While government disaster response systems have significantly strengthened over the years, many communities continue to experience gaps in receiving timely life-saving rescue and relief due to damaged infrastructure, geographical isolation and limited local response capacities during the critical first few hours after a disaster. These challenges highlight the urgent need for locally rooted, youth-led response mechanisms that can bridge physical access gaps and complement government efforts.
There is a growing need to build and sustain trained, empowered and equipped local Youth Response Units, Circle Quick Response Teams (CQRTs) and Village Disaster Management Committees (VDMCs) that can act immediately when disasters strike. Beyond emergency response, communities require long-term investments in prevention, preparedness and recovery to reduce risks and strengthen resilience. Children and young people also need opportunities to develop the skills, health and leadership capacities that will enable them to become resilient citizens and future disaster leaders.
Joti Foundation is uniquely positioned to address these needs by combining community ownership, youth empowerment, modern technology and strong partnerships with NDRF, SDRFs and local authorities. Through this integrated approach, JF will contribute to saving lives, reducing disaster impacts and strengthening resilient communities across the complete Disaster Management Cycle.
SWOT Analysis
Joti Foundation conducted a SWOT (Strengths, Weaknesses, Opportunities and Threats) analysis in June 2026 to assess its current institutional position and identify strategic priorities for the period 2026–2036.
Strengths
Authentic and compassionate leadership that demonstrates strong commitment and action during emergencies.
Proven operational capacity to deliver timely rescue and relief, particularly during the critical first 24–72 hours of a disaster, as demonstrated in Punjab floods.
Strong field implementation capability through an effective and motivated ground team.
Demonstrated Search and Rescue expertise, including the ability to bridge physical access gaps and reach isolated disaster-affected communities.
Established credibility and relationships with key institutions, including the National Disaster Response Force (NDRF), Government of Punjab, SEEDS and Start Network.
Strong multi-sector partnerships with government agencies, CSR partners, academic institutions such as the Indian Institute of Hydrology, and other organisations.
Youth-led organisational identity, creating opportunities for long-term community ownership and sustainability.

Weaknesses
Limited institutional experience in Disaster Risk Reduction (DRR), although this gap is actively being addressed through capacity-building initiatives.
Organisational systems and policies are still evolving, including governance, administration and operational procedures.
Vision, mission and long-term strategic direction are newly being formalised.
Corporate partnerships are concentrated among a limited number of CSR partners, needing diversification.
Professional memberships and accreditations (e.g., Sphere, CHS Alliance, Impact Hub TISS and ADRN) have just been initiated.
Knowledge management and publications are not yet systematically developed to capture and disseminate field learning.
Foreign funding limitations due to the pending Foreign Contribution Regulation Act (FCRA) registration.

Opportunities
Establishing Youth Response Units (YRUs), Circle Quick Response Teams (CQRTs) and Village Disaster Management Committees (VDMCs) as sustainable community institutions.
Scaling Search and Rescue operations nationally across multiple disaster-prone states.
Supporting updates to State Disaster Management Plans (SDMPs) by contributing field-based evidence and lessons learned.
Building formal partnerships with State Disaster Management Authorities (SDMAs), beginning with Assam and expanding to other states.
Strengthening engagement with NDMA through strategic partnerships and policy contributions.
Partnering with adventure, mountaineering and water-safety institutions to build specialised Search and Rescue capacities.
Building partnerships with international Search and Rescue experts and humanitarian organisations.
Diversifying resource mobilisation opportunities, including CSR partnerships, philanthropy, national and international foundations, high-net-worth individuals (HNIs), diaspora communities and crowdfunding.
Developing innovative sustainability mechanisms, including income-generating assets, charitable events and corporate employee engagement programmes.
Leveraging government programmes and schemes to strengthen disaster resilience and community preparedness.

Threats
Community-based Search and Rescue operations inherently carry risks of injury or loss of life for responders and affected populations.
Extreme weather events and increasingly complex disasters may exceed local response capacities.
Overdependence on a small number of funding sources may affect programme sustainability.
Delays in obtaining regulatory approvals, including FCRA registration, may limit access to international funding opportunities.
Volunteer attrition and youth migration from rural areas may affect the long-term sustainability of local response units.
Risk Mitigation Measures
JF will mitigate these risks by investing in rigorous training and certification of youth responders, ensuring all rescue equipment meets safety standards and is periodically inspected, incorporating trained paramedics within response teams, strengthening partnerships with NDRF, SDRF and local authorities, and diversifying funding sources to ensure long-term institutional sustainability.
Strategic implication: The SWOT analysis confirms that JF's comparative advantage lies in its youth-led, community-based Search and Rescue model, which should remain at the centre of its growth strategy while institutional systems, partnerships and resource mobilisation capacities are strengthened over time.
Strategic Context
India is among the world's most disaster-prone countries, experiencing recurrent floods, cyclones, earthquakes, landslides, heatwaves and other climate-related hazards that are increasing in frequency, intensity and complexity. Rapid urbanisation, environmental degradation, population growth and climate change are further amplifying the vulnerability of communities, particularly those living in remote, riverine, mountainous and socio-economically disadvantaged areas. Despite significant investments by the Government of India in strengthening disaster management systems, many communities continue to face challenges in accessing timely rescue and relief during the critical first hours following a disaster.
Joti Foundation recognises that building resilient communities requires more than emergency response alone. Sustainable resilience demands investments across the complete Disaster Management Cycle, including Prevention and Mitigation, Preparedness, Response and Recovery, while placing communities and young people at the centre of action. JF's strategic approach aligns with the National Disaster Management Act and complements the work of the National Disaster Management Authority (NDMA), NDRF, State Disaster Response Forces (SDRFs) and local authorities. Roshan Punjab (Children's Eye Health)
Roshan Punjab is Joti Foundation's flagship children's eye health programme that strengthens long-term resilience by improving children's vision and learning outcomes. The programme screens school-going children for vision impairments, trains teachers to conduct basic screenings, promotes self-testing through visual charts in schools and facilitates the provision of spectacles where required. Implemented in partnership with the Government of Punjab, the programme has already reached approximately one million children, helping them build healthier and more resilient futures.



Flood Response
Flood Response is JF's emergency response programme focused on delivering life-saving rescue and relief during the critical first few hours of a disaster. Through trained youth responders, rescue boats and close coordination with NDRF, SDRF and local authorities, JF bridges physical access barriers to reach isolated communities and ensure timely assistance when every minute matters.

Climate Action (Groundwater and Drinking Water-Jal Jeevan)
The Climate Action programme strengthens community resilience by promoting groundwater conservation, sustainable water management and climate adaptation measures. The programme supports corporates and communities to better prepare for the increasing impacts of climate change through awareness, local action and practical solutions that protect natural resources and strengthen long-term water security. Jal Jeevan aims to improve access to safe and sustainable drinking water in vulnerable communities. The programme promotes water security through community-based solutions, awareness, infrastructure support and partnerships with local institutions. By improving access to clean water, JF contributes to better health outcomes, reduced vulnerabilities and stronger community resilience before, during and after disasters.
Drawing on its field experience during the Punjab floods, JF has demonstrated that empowered local youth, equipped with appropriate skills, modern technology and rescue equipment, can bridge physical access barriers and save lives when every minute matters. During 2026–2036, JF will build a scalable youth-led disaster resilience model across priority states, contribute evidence and lessons to strengthen policies, and mobilise partnerships to create safer, more resilient communities across India.
Vision
By 2036, an India where disaster-prone communities are resilient, prepared, and connected, with empowered community youth harnessing modern technology, leading timely life-saving actions, bridging critical access gaps to affected communities, and building safer, stronger, and more resilient futures for all.
A few words about our Vision
During the pre-disaster period, JF will work on prevention and mitigation measures (e.g., strengthening river embankments or retrofitting a building) to avoid or reduce the impact of a disaster. After the disaster, post-rescue and relief, JF will work on Building Back Better (BBB), which means building infrastructure (damaged in the disaster) that is better and stronger than before. While we shall learn from the government policies in our execution, we shall offer inputs for strengthening them from the lessons learned.
Mission
Build, empower, and equip community youth response units to deliver life-saving rescue and immediate relief within the critical 24 hours after a disaster in tandem with NDRF, SDRF, and local authorities, while strengthening community capacities to anticipate, prevent, prepare for, respond to, and recover from disasters. 
A few words about our Mission
Joti Foundation's mission is to build a strong network of community-based Youth Response Units (YRUs) in line with CQRT - Circle Quick Response Team that can act as the first line of support during disasters, especially within the critical 24 hours of a disaster when every minute matters. JF will work in close alignment with government disaster management systems, including NDRF, SDRF, district administrations and local authorities, while contributing field-based learning to strengthen national disaster management policies and practices.
At the village level, JF will work with and strengthen Village Disaster Management Committees (VDMCs) and will be supported by the YRUs. These youth-led volunteer groups will be equipped with survival skills, emergency response competencies, leadership abilities and modern technologies to support prevention, preparedness, rescue, relief and recovery efforts in their own communities.
Over the next decade, JF will prioritise intensive work in four disaster-prone focus regions: the North Eastern states, Uttarakhand, Himachal Pradesh and Punjab. In parallel, JF will undertake preparedness interventions in additional states, including Gujarat (earthquakes and cyclones) and Andhra Pradesh (floods and cyclones), with the flexibility to elevate these states to focus areas as needs and capacities evolve.
Beyond state-level interventions, JF will work at the national level by aligning its programmes with the National Disaster Management Act and related government frameworks, while systematically documenting and sharing lessons from the field to strengthen India's disaster management systems and promote Build Back Better (BBB) approaches across disaster-affected communities.

Theory of Change
Joti Foundation believes that every life can be saved when communities have timely access to rescue and relief and are empowered to build resilience before, during and after disasters.
If Joti Foundation builds, empowers and equips local Youth Response Units (YRUs), Circle Quick Response Teams (CQRTs) and Village Disaster Management Committees (VDMCs); invests in youth leadership, skills, livelihoods and modern technology; and works in partnership with NDRF, SDRFs, local authorities and communities throughout the complete Disaster Management Cycle, then disaster-prone communities will become better prepared, physical access barriers will be overcome, rescue and relief will reach affected populations at the right time, and communities will recover faster and become more resilient.
This is because local youth are often the first people present when disasters strike and are uniquely positioned to act immediately, particularly in remote and hard-to-reach locations where external responders may take time to arrive. By strengthening local capacities before disasters occur and embedding youth-led response systems within communities, JF can complement government efforts and create sustainable, locally owned disaster resilience systems.
Over time, this approach will contribute to a safer and more resilient India where lives are protected, communities are empowered and lessons learned from the field continuously strengthen disaster management policies and practices.


Strategic Goals

Local Disaster Response Systems: Establish and sustain at least 1,000 local Youth Response Units (YRUs), 400 Circle Quick Response Teams (CQRTs) and 2,000 Village Disaster Management Committees (VDMCs) across the disaster-prone districts in India by 2036.
Timely Search, Rescue and Relief: By 2036, ensure that all JF-supported districts have trained youth response units capable of delivering life-saving rescue and relief within the first 24 hours of a disaster through a national network of YRUs (approximately 5,000 youth).
Climate-Resilient Communities: By 2036, build resilient communities in at least 50 disaster-prone districts by implementing programmes across all four phases of the Disaster Management Cycle: Prevention and Mitigation, Preparedness, Response and Recovery.
Disaster Management Policies and Systems: Assist policymakers in refining Disaster Management policies annually by bringing grassroots lessons and innovations to the decision-making table. (E.g., search & rescue-related policies, overall DM related policies).
Strategic Partnerships and Resource Mobilisation: By 2036, establish strategic partnerships with government agencies, CSR partners, philanthropic institutions, national and international foundations and communities to mobilise sustainable resources that strengthen JF's long-term impact and institutional resilience.

Cross-cutting Themes (GEDSI)

Joti Foundation recognises that disasters disproportionately affect women, children, older persons, persons with disabilities and socially excluded populations. Building resilient communities therefore requires an intentional commitment to Gender Equality, Disability and Social Inclusion (GEDSI) across all aspects of planning, implementation, monitoring and learning.

GEDSI is not a standalone programme but a cross-cutting principle that will be embedded across all four flagship programmes, institutional systems and partnerships. JF will ensure that vulnerable and underrepresented groups are not only protected during disasters but are also empowered to actively participate in preparedness, response and recovery processes.
Particular emphasis will be placed on promoting meaningful participation and leadership of women, youth and persons with disabilities (where appropriate) within Youth Response Units (YRUs), Circle Quick Response Teams (CQRTs) and Village Disaster Management Committees (VDMCs). Training materials, communication systems, rescue equipment and community awareness activities will be designed to be accessible, inclusive and context-appropriate.
JF will adopt a “leave no one behind” approach by identifying and addressing barriers related to geography, gender, age, disability, language, socio-economic status and social exclusion. Data will be disaggregated by sex, age and disability wherever feasible to ensure evidence-based decision-making and equitable programme outcomes.
By embedding GEDSI across the complete Disaster Management Cycle, JF aims to build communities that are safer, more equitable, more resilient and better prepared to withstand future disasters.

JF will also cultivate strategic partnerships with CSR partners, philanthropic institutions, academic and technical institutions, civil society organisations and international humanitarian networks. Through these collaborations, JF will mobilise financial, technical and knowledge resources, while contributing grassroots lessons and innovations to strengthen disaster management policies and practices. JF will adopt a strategic approach to resource mobilisation by building partnerships with government agencies, CSR partners, philanthropic institutions, national and international foundations, high-net-worth individuals, communities and individual donors. Organisational Structure

Joti Foundation adopts a lean, collaborative and mission-driven organisational structure designed to provide strong governance, strategic leadership and effective programme delivery. 
The Board of Trustees is the highest governing body of Joti Foundation, ultimately responsible for the organisation's health and growth.  The Board of Directors is the second-highest governing body and is collectively responsible for providing strategic direction, ensuring good governance and safeguarding the organisation's mission, values and long-term sustainability. Both of these Boards focus on governance rather than day-to-day management, delegating operational leadership to the Managing Trustee and Chief Executive Officer while providing strategic oversight, guidance and accountability. Furthermore, an independent Advisory Board offers technical expertise and external perspectives. Based on the key expertise of each of the members, their invididual terms of reference will be developed to define their roles and responsibilities or specific tasks.  The Strategic Leadership Team is responsible for translating the Strategic Plan into action and ensuring organisational performance. 
Two operational pillars support implementation: the Programme Management Team, which leads programme design and field execution, and the Operations Management Team, which oversees finance, administration, human resources, communications, IT and institutional systems. This structure enables JF to remain agile, youth-led and responsive while strengthening accountability, partnerships and long-term organisational sustainability.

These institutional strategic goals will ensure that JF remains youth-led, agile and responsive, while maintaining high standards of governance, operational excellence and long-term resilience. MEAL Framework

Joti Foundation recognises that achieving its vision requires a strong culture of evidence, accountability and continuous improvement. The MEAL Framework will provide a systematic approach to monitor progress, measure outcomes, strengthen accountability and capture lessons learned across all programmes and institutional functions.
MEAL will be integrated across all flagship programmes and strategic goals, enabling JF to track progress, improve decision-making and strengthen its contribution to national disaster resilience efforts.
Monitoring
JF will establish standard indicators, targets and reporting systems to regularly monitor programme implementation, outputs and outcomes at community, district, state and national levels.
Evaluation
Periodic internal and external evaluations will assess programme effectiveness, efficiency, relevance, sustainability and impact, and inform future programme design.
Accountability
JF will establish transparent feedback and grievance mechanisms to ensure that communities, youth responders, partners, staff and donors can safely provide inputs and raise concerns.
Learning
JF will systematically document field experiences, innovations, success stories and lessons learned to strengthen organisational practices and contribute to disaster management policies and systems.
