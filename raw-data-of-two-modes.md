Joti Foundation’s story began long before it had a name, with two generations of a family quietly serving
the communities around them. Their belief was simple yet enduring: every girl deserves an education,
every local artisan access to fair markets, every farmer the knowledge to prosper and every young person
the opportunity to choose a hopeful path. Over time, these individual acts of service grew into the shared
purpose that became Joti Foundation.
Women, children and youth are at the heart of our work, shaping priorities and helping lead change within
their communities. Through education, eye health, youth development, livelihoods, safe water and
women’s empowerment, we work to expand opportunities and strengthen self-reliance. Our approach
rests on one enduring belief: lasting change grows from standing alongside communities, recognising their
strengths and supporting their aspirations.

CHILDREN’S EYE HEALTH
— ROSHAN PUNJAB (Community impact in Punjab)
For a child, a pair of spectacles can mean far more than clearer sight; it can restore confidence,
classroom participation and the ability to learn. Yet childhood vision problems often go unrecognised,
with difficulty concentrating or declining classroom performance mistaken for a lack of interest in
learning. Implemented under a formal MoU with the Government of Punjab, Roshan Punjab brings
eye care closer to children through school-based screening, teacher engagement, optometrist
assessments, referral pathways and corrective spectacles.
During Phase I, the programme screened 353,349 children across five districts and distributed
30,723 corrective spectacles, including 22,121 to first-time wearers. A further 1,871 children were
referred for assessment and care. The programme also reached 6,407 truck drivers and community
members, including targeted vision support for drivers to promote safer roads.
Building on this reach, Phase II will expand to two additional districts through trained teacher Vision
Champions and stronger links with Rashtriya Bal Swasthya Karyakram, District Early Intervention
Centres and government health facilities. Vision Walls, systematic follow-up and periodic re-
screening will help schools sustain early identification and timely care beyond the project period.

MERA PIND MERI KHED (Community impact in Punjab)
We are building a youth-led sports ecosystem across 60 villages of Sri Muktsar Sahib, aiming to reach
over 10,000 young people and create spaces where they can play, belong, lead and aspire. Particular
emphasis is placed on adolescent girls and young women, enabling their participation through safe,
inclusive spaces and equal opportunities to train, compete and lead.
With locally rooted Khel Saathis, regular sporting engagement, coaching and pathways from village
grounds to competitive leagues, sport becomes a means to nurture discipline, confidence, teamwork
and leadership. Beyond the playground, youth are encouraged to grow as informed and responsible
citizens, with exposure to constitutional values, civic responsibility and career pathways. They will
also actively participate in local development, helping identify village priorities and contributing to
community initiatives.
The ambition is simple: to turn village playgrounds into grounds where stronger futures and future
leaders are shaped.

ECONOMIC
INDEPENDENCE AMONG
RURAL WOMEN: (Community impact in Punjab)
From Craft to Confidence
We assist rural women through self-help groups, enabling them to access credit, strengthen
enterprise-management skills and build reliable supply chains that connect their craftsmanship with
wider markets. By helping women secure fairer value for their work, we seek to transform traditional
skills into sustainable livelihoods and create pathways towards greater financial independence. As
women gain greater economic agency, they are better positioned to participate in household
decisions and invest in their families’ education, nutrition, healthcare and overall well-being. Beyond
improving incomes, this intervention strengthens women’s confidence, dignity and leadership,
enabling them to shape more secure and hopeful futures for themselves, their families and their
communities.
Through a dedicated boutique in Chandigarh, we have connected a growing group of women artisans
with customers and created a platform for their products to reach wider markets. Building on this
experience, we are now working to nurture entrepreneurship among rural women in Sri Muktsar
Sahib district by strengthening business skills, product development, quality standards, financial
literacy and market linkages. Our aim is to enable women to progress from skilled producers to
confident entrepreneurs who can manage sustainable enterprises, generate reliable incomes and
create opportunities for other women within their communities.

MENSTRUAL HYGIENE
PROGRAMME (Community impact in Punjab)
Safety and Dignity for Every Woman
Menstruation is a natural part of life, yet silence, stigma and misinformation continue to prevent
many women and adolescent girls from managing it safely and with dignity. In Sukma, Chhattisgarh,
we worked with tribal communities to address menstrual-health challenges through awareness,
practical guidance and access to appropriate menstrual products. The initiative created safe spaces
for women and girls to discuss menstruation openly, challenge harmful myths and make informed
choices about their health. Participants were also provided with free reusable menstrual products and
guidance on their safe use and care.
Building on this experience, we continue our menstrual health and hygiene work in Sri Muktsar Sahib
district, Punjab. The programme engages women and adolescent girls through community-based
awareness, promotes safe hygiene practices and works to reduce the shame and social restrictions
associated with menstruation. By combining knowledge, access and dialogue, the initiative seeks to
ensure that every woman and girl can manage menstruation safely, confidently and with dignity.

SAFE DRINKING WATER (Community impact in Punjab)
We approach water security as both a development priority and a cornerstone of climate resilience,
bringing together access to safe drinking water, community purification systems, groundwater awareness
and sustainable water management. The Foundation has mobilised and deployed six water-purification
units valued at approximately Rs. 40 lakh in Punjab. This commitment extends into emergencies: mobile
community water-purification systems, known as Instawater, were deployed during the Assam and Bihar
floods to provide safe drinking water where conventional supply systems had been disrupted. These
systems help protect public health, preserve dignity and maintain access to an essential service when
vulnerable communities face an increased risk of waterborne diseases. By reducing dependence on
single-use packaged drinking-water bottles during emergencies, this approach also lessens the
environmental footprint of humanitarian response meeting an urgent human need without creating an
additional environmental burden.

ASSAM FLOOD RESPONSE 2026 (Emergency response across India)
In Upper Assam’s Sivasagar district, Joti Foundation mounted a community-centred flood response,
supporting families as they moved from prolonged displacement towards return and recovery.
Working alongside local volunteers, community representatives and government authorities, we
supplied over 100,000 litres of safe drinking water and provided food, household essentials, shelter
and hygiene items to 2,450 families. Across 11 villages, our teams also supported the cleaning of
homes and public spaces and promoted hygiene practices to help families restore safer living
conditions. Assistance prioritised households facing the greatest unmet needs, including women
managing households alone, older people, persons with disabilities and families who had lost their
homes and essential belongings. Community participation helped guide the response, keeping
assistance grounded in local priorities and the dignity of those receiving it.
Responsible relief shaped both how assistance reached families and what it left behind. Mobile water
purification units reduced dependence on packaged drinking water, providing a community-scale
solution while limiting plastic waste. Coordination with the Public Health Engineering Department
supported water-quality testing, while local volunteers helped reach families facing barriers to access.
Alongside relief delivery, plastic waste was collected, segregated and channelled for recycling in
coordination with local authorities and communities. These efforts reflected a simple commitment:
urgent humanitarian assistance should protect people without adding to the environmental burden
they face. As immediate needs gave way to the longer work of recovery, our focus remained on
helping families regain safer living conditions, rebuild their everyday routines and move forward with
dignity.

BIHAR FLOOD RESPONSE 2026 (Emergency response across India)
In Bihar, our intervention began with anticipatory action, deploying teams and local volunteers to
Munger and Khagaria after forecasts indicated rising flood risk. Communities were supported with
early warnings, safer evacuation, movement of livestock, protection of essential documents and
identification of vulnerable households. As flooding intensified, the response transitioned to
humanitarian assistance, reaching 500 affected families with essential food supplies, hygiene kits and
hygiene promotion, shelter support, 8 villages with safe drinking water through a mobile purification
unit and the cleaning of contaminated handpumps. By connecting early action with timely relief, the
intervention helped families prepare before the crisis, remain safer during the floods and take their
first steps towards recovery with dignity.
